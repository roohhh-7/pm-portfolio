KitchenMate: Solving Choice Overload

Product Strategy · Gemini 1.5 Pro · UX Design
Problem Statement
Urban households face structural decision fatigue. While 57% of households report daily stress regarding meal choices, most apps fail because manual inventory tracking feels like a chore. The gap isn't a lack of recipes; it's the cognitive load of selection. Even motivated cooks default to takeout because translating fridge contents into a plan requires mental bandwidth they don't have after a workday.
User Personas
💼
Depleted Rahul
Professional, 28
Low mental bandwidth after work; wants a zero-thought solution that uses what's in the fridge.
"I'm too tired to think—just tell me what I can cook in 20 mins."
👪
Efficient Ananya
Busy Parent, 35
Needs to feed a family quickly without manual inventory checking.
"I have ingredients, but no plan. Help me turn this grocery bag into dinner."
Solution & How It Works
KitchenMate uses Gemini 1.5 Pro + Computer Vision to bridge the gap between pantry inventory and daily action.
1
Smart Sync: User photographs grocery receipts to auto-populate the digital pantry.
2
AI Curation: Gemini parses ingredients, expiration dates, and user preferences to find matching recipes.
3
3 Meals Now: To avoid choice paralysis (Hick's Law), the app presents exactly three curated meal options.
4
Guided Prep: Delivers hands-free, voice-guided instructions to move the user from decision to plate.

Key Design Decisions & Tradeoffs

Choice Limitation over Exploration: Decided to limit suggestions to exactly 3 meals to solve paralysis, even if users might want more variety. This prioritizes "time-to-action" over discovery.

Automated Tracking over Manual Accuracy: Prioritized receipt scanning for speed, accepting that some niche items might need manual correction. Acceptable tradeoff for user retention.

Decision-First over Content-First: The UI focuses on "what to cook now" rather than browsing a library of recipes. Sacrifice of traditional engagement for utility.

Ayurvedic Digital Twin Scheduler

HealthTech · Workflow Automation · Optimization
Problem Statement
Ayurvedic clinical workflows, particularly multi-day Panchakarma programs, are plagued by scheduling entropy. Manual coordination of therapists, rooms, and strict clinical protocols leads to "ghost" appointments (staff not present) and timing violations (e.g., morning-specific treatments scheduled in the afternoon). The gap isn't just a calendar; it's the lack of real-time clinical context and verified staff presence.
User Personas
👩‍⚕️

Head Physician, 48
Ensuring strict protocol adherence without manual spreadsheet management.
"I need protocols followed perfectly without spending 4 hours on scheduling."
👨‍💼
Sanjay
Clinic Admin, 35
Managing staff and room occupancy with real-time attendance changes.
"Managing 30 therapists is impossible when attendance changes hourly."
Solution & How It Works
A real-time scheduling engine that synchronizes physical clinic assets with biometric staff presence.
1
Biometric Sync: Live staff availability is verified via check-ins, eliminating "ghost" slots.
2
Digital Twin Replica: Creates a virtual live-map of rooms, equipment, and staff for conflict detection.
3
Review-Aware Matching: Pairs patients with therapists based on ratings and seniority.
4
Protocol Locks: Automatically enforces time-locked clinical rules (e.g., Morning-only treatments).

Swiggy Instamart: The Smarter Checkout Dilemma

Product Strategy · Behavioral Psychology · E-Commerce UX
Problem Statement

Many Instamart carts fall just short of the free-delivery threshold. Users typically respond by scrolling to add cheap junk items or abandoning the cart. For example, one user noted they would: "scroll around trying to find some random pack of chips or close the app." This is poor UX and hurts conversion.

Solution & How It Works

Swiggy introduced a clever tweak allowing users to add the exact shortfall to their Swiggy Money wallet at checkout to unlock free delivery. Instead of forcing users to pile on unwanted items, Swiggy lets them pay the gap as wallet credit.

1
Cart Check: User is at checkout. System checks if cart ≥ free-delivery threshold.
2
Wallet Prompt: If below threshold, Swiggy displays the wallet-topup option ("Add ₹X for free delivery").
3
User Choice: If added, delivery charge is waived and ₹X is added as Swiggy Money for future use. The user feels they've retained value rather than losing it to a fee.
Behavioral Psychology Mechanisms
🧠 Mental Accounting: Converts a "lost" fee into prepaid groceries.
💼 Endowment Effect: Unused wallet cash feels like a personal asset.
🔒 Commitment Device: Preloading creates incentive to return.
⚡ Instant Gratification: "FREE Delivery at ZERO Cost!" triggers immediate reward.
Expected Business Impact
+5%
Cart Conversion
+5%

~20%

Key Risks & Mitigations

Unit-Economics Hit on Small Orders: Free delivery is costly on low-basket orders. Swiggy bears full delivery cost on unprofitable orders if not balanced. Mitigation: Limit to very small gaps (e.g. <₹50) or require a minimum cart value.

Customer Trust: Users might wonder if Swiggy is forcing them into a wallet or concerned about hidden tricks. Mitigation: Clear messaging ("Redeem fully on your next order").

Low Usage of Credit: If users forget their credit, they may feel their money is "locked." Mitigation: App reminders to spend balance or allow refund of small balances.

Veil: The Privacy-First Video Editor

On-Device ML · Video Redaction · UX Design
Problem Statement

In an era of ubiquitous video sharing, protecting privacy remains a high-friction, deeply technical task. While 74% of users express concern about digital privacy, the majority still share unredacted videos because existing mobile video editors make redaction a manual, frame-by-frame ordeal.

Tools that offer automated tracking require users to upload their raw footage to a cloud server, presenting an unacceptable security risk.

User Personas
📹
Sarah, Independent Journalist
Needs to protect sources immediately before uploading footage.
"I can't risk uploading raw footage to a cloud just to blur a face."
👨‍👧
David, Careful Parent
Wants to blur out school logos or background children before sharing.
"Editing on my phone is too hard, I just end up not sharing the video."
Solution & How It Works

Veil reframes video redaction from a 'professional editing task' to a single-tap privacy action. By leveraging on-device Machine Learning (ML Kit) for automated tracking, Veil allows users to identify and mask sensitive information locally, reducing 'Time-to-Redact' from hours to seconds with zero cloud dependency.

1
Scan Privacy: On-device ML scans the video in seconds, extracting faces and objects.
2
Decision: Presents a clean list of detected faces & text ("Inbox" model).
3
One-Tap Blur: One tap applies a dynamic mosaic tracking the face across frames.

Product Retrospective & Key Learnings

The "Black Box" Trust Issue: Initially, the app just applied blurs automatically, which users hated due to lack of control. Solution: Shifted to an "Inbox" model where the app lists "Tracked Items" for the user to explicitly tap 'Apply Blur'.

UI Simplicity vs. Power User Needs: Power users wanted keyframes; casual users were overwhelmed. Solution: Abstracted complexity. Tapping an item snaps the timeline to it, prioritizing status buttons (Blur, Emoji) over sliders.

The Cost of Local Processing: Running ML and FFmpeg on-device is heavy. Takeaway: Aggressively optimized frame extraction using low-res proxies for ML, keeping final export native. Speed of UI took priority over real-time playback res.

Aegis: Visual Workflow Builder

FinTech · Risk Management · Workflow Builder
The Problem Space

In the modern FinTech and e-commerce landscape, fraud patterns evolve rapidly while internal risk teams are often stuck using legacy, code-heavy systems.

Through continuous discovery, we identified three critical bottlenecks:

  • The Engineering Chokepoint: Implementing a new rule requires engineering tickets, sprint prioritization, and deploying code, costing millions in realized fraud.
  • The "Black Box" Problem: Legacy rule engines rely on complex nested logic that is difficult to visualize or audit.
  • The Cost of False Positives: Deploying rules without robust testing blocks legitimate customers. A 1% increase in false positives can devastate revenue.
Target Audience & Jobs-to-be-Done
🛡️
Alex, Fraud Strategy Manager
Needs to react to fraud attacks immediately without waiting for engineering.
"I need to adjust velocity checks instantly to stop a sudden attack."
📊
Sam, Risk Analyst
Needs to backtest theories against real-world data at scale.
"I must prove this new rule won't block our best customers before it goes live."
💻
Jordan, Software Engineer
Wants to stop updating basic threshold values and focus on core architecture.
"Risk configuration changes take up 30% of my week."
The Solution & Vision

The vision for Aegis was to create the "Figma for Fraud Prevention." An interactive workspace that shifts power back to domain experts while ensuring engineering safety.

1
Build Visually: A node-based canvas (React Flow) to construct complex logic with zero cognitive overhead.
2
Test Instantly: High-performance simulation backend to run workflows against massive historical datasets.
3
Analyze Deeply: An analytics suite providing immediate feedback (e.g., Revenue Protected, Approval Rate).

Challenges & Trade-offs

Information Density vs. Usability: Complex nodes threatened to clutter the canvas. Solution: Implemented collapsible sidebars, robust search, and a minimalist node design to keep the workspace focused.

Performance at Scale: Simulating thousands of transactions in the browser caused UI lag. Solution: Decoupled UI from simulation logic, building a lightweight Python backend to handle heavy computation asynchronously.

Future Roadmap (V2)
  • Live Shadow Mode: Testing rules against real-time Kafka feeds without affecting actual customer outcomes.
  • Generative AI Integration: Auto-generating node graphs using LLMs (e.g., "Create a rule to block >$500 from high-risk countries").
  • Enterprise Collaboration: Multi-player mode for commenting, version history, and deployment approvals.
